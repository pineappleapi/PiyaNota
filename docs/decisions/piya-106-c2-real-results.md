## How to read the numbers

Arm 1 is the whole-strip arm, printed first and not a chosen headline (pitch recall and token SER, header included in SER). Arm 3 is the bar-masked body score (header stripped, masked bars dropped). Arm 2 is the pitch-only floor. Header mode `gt` takes the clef and key from the ground truth, so it isolates note reading. Header mode `own` takes the clef and key from the prediction, so a wrong header costs pitch. Every pitch number below sits beside the label-only control floors from PREREG item 6 (they depend only on the labels and the kept strips, so they apply to camera unchanged): constant prediction 8.7% / 4.7% / 8.8% (Arm 1 / Arm 3 / Arm 2), shuffled prediction 18.7% / 12.0% / 18.7%, every step shifted 21.4% / 13.2% / 21.8%.

## Separating the filter from ClefKeyNet

ClefKeyNet leaves `gt`-header pitch unchanged, because the header is replaced by the ground truth there. All of the following ClefKeyNet-versus-filter figures are descriptive, with no paired interval. Its Arm 1 token SER is 0.2 pp higher than the filter's (4.8 against 4.6 on PDF, 5.3 against 5.1 on camera). ## The camera rebuild mismatch (Amendment 2)

In ClefKeyNet mode on camera, 234 of 912 strips rebuilt from the photo do not match the frozen strip within the Amendment 1 tolerance, in all ten pieces, for both seeds. The runner flagged them (`rebuild_mismatch`) and read ClefKeyNet from the current page and box. Strip-level key accuracy against the labels' header is 96.9 percent on the 608 unflagged strips (589) and 95.3 percent on the 213 flagged strips with a key label (203), the same in both seeds. (A recount with the scorer's own header function gave 590 of 608 and 204 of 213, each one higher; the gap of about 1.2 to 1.6 pp is the same conclusion.) That gap is about two strips of 213 and is not distinguishable from noise at this size; it is absence of evidence, not proof of no effect. The flag cannot change ClefKeyNet's input, which is the current page and box; what it records is that ClefKeyNet and the CRNN read different crops of the same staff. Arm 1 on the camera set split by the flag (212 flagged and 604 unflagged strips in Arm 1; pitch % and token SER %, `own` header, seed 1 / seed 2): filter, flagged 96.5 / 95.6 pitch and 4.8 / 5.0 SER, unflagged 96.2 / 95.9 pitch and 5.2 / 5.2 SER; ClefKeyNet, flagged 96.1 / 95.2 pitch and 5.0 / 5.2 SER, unflagged 95.7 / 95.5 pitch and 5.3 / 5.3 SER. The ClefKeyNet minus filter pitch difference is −0.4 / −0.3 pp on flagged strips and −0.5 / −0.4 pp on unflagged strips. No difference between the groups is visible, but no interval was computed and the piece mix differs (Kay_Ganda, where ClefKeyNet loses most, is 59 of 212 flagged strips and 73 of 604 unflagged), so this does not establish that the flag is harmless. The key-accuracy counts above cover the 821 strips with a key label across all arms; Arm 1 has 816. The list of flagged strips is in `D:/PiyaNota/data/c2_real_scores/camera_rebuild_mismatch_strips.json`. The cause is only partly found. Partial mechanism, reproduced for Ale page 1 box 1 only: `_extract_boxes` truncates YOLO float coordinates to integers, and that box's y1 is 351.011, 0.011 px above an integer boundary, so a tiny float difference between sessions flips it to 350. `_content_extent` (stage2_detection.py:457) builds the crop from connected components in integer rows with neighbour limits, a step function, so one row changes the strip width (1353 against 1372). Ale page 1 box 2 and the other 232 strips are not explained and were not tested. This is consistent with crop sensitivity rather than a code bug, but the cause of 233 of the 234 strips is unknown, and the widths in Ale page 1 box 1 are rebuilt 1372 against frozen 1353. A second hypothesis (the later crop and dewarp mapping to native resolution) is not excluded. Implication: about 26 percent of camera strips are not reproducible across sessions, so all camera numbers are conditional on the frozen cut, and the stability of the cut is unmeasured; a fresh pipeline run on the same photos would feed the CRNN different crops for those strips. The checks that ruled out other causes: the Stage 1 and Stage 2 sources have the cut record's hashes, the cutter called preprocessing with the same arguments, the box centres equal the cut record's centres to 0.0001 of the page height (Ale page 1, so detection is not the difference), CPU and GPU boxes agree, and two rebuilds in a row agree. On Ale page 1 the 12 rebuilt strip widths equal the frozen ones in 10 cases and differ in two (system 1, staves 1 and 2), which points at the later crop and dewarp mapping to native resolution. The CRNN reads only the frozen strips, so the raw and filter rows do not depend on this.

## What this does and does not show

It describes how the two C2 checkpoints read the 10 test pieces, with the ground truth as the reference. It does not compare C2 with A7 (no improvement claim), it does not show the pipeline works end to end (the scorer reads strips cut with the frozen cut, not Stage 4 output), and the stated intervals cover strips of 10 pieces. The ClefKeyNet gate remains failed (BUG-021); this set does not change that. Meter correctness is per-strip, not per-bar, and mid-strip key or meter changes are handled only as the CRNN reads them (the runner applies the system-start filter and key continuity, not the mid-strip rule or any meter rule).

## Known limitation: independent two-voice divisi (user decision 2026-11-08)

Strips whose label contains `layer.split` (two voices on one staff with different rhythms) read much worse than ordinary strips. Seed 1, PDF, raw CRNN, `own` header, Arm 1 pitch recall, split by `layer.split` in the label after the results were seen (exploratory, not pre-registered):

| Piece | Ordinary strips | Layer strips | Layer share of notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ama_Namin | 99.4% (330 notes) | 34.0% (153 notes, 7 strips) | 32% |
| Pangarap_Ko_Ang_Ibigin_Ka | 98.1% | 67.6% (170) | 11% |
| Pangarap_Lang | 96.6% | 74.9% (179) | 9% |
| May_Hihigit_Pa_Ba | 99.3% | 73.9% (23) | 3% |
| Gaano_Ko_Ikaw_Kamahal | 99.9% | 85.7% (28) | 4% |
| Ale | 99.7% | 92.7% (109) | 7% |
| Bahay_Kubo | 100.0% | 98.0% (343) | 27% |

Ama_Namin's 78.7% pooled pitch is explained by this: its ordinary strips score 99.4%. Kay_Ganda, Paru-parong_Bukid and Tikong_Tiko have no layer strips. Same-rhythm `chordnote` chords read well (the A9/C2 x-order labels); Bahay_Kubo's layer strips scoring 98% suggests layer type is a confound, but its layer strips were not checked token by token.

Caveats: Ama_Namin's 153 layer notes lie on 7 correlated strips, so the effective sample is about 7. Set/LCS recall may flatter flattened-voice output. Density, ledger lines and strip height are uncontrolled.

Status: not fixed and not planned for this project. Earlier attempts are recorded in memory and the design docs: A7's layer arm read 20 to 23 percent of `layer.split` tokens, with layer bars about 0.8 percent of training bars; A9 dropped layers; A8b (oversample layer bars 4 to 5 times) was never run. ml-cv-lead's opinion (2026-11-08, no files read): CTC on one flattened sequence is structurally weak for independent rhythms, A8b has about a 15 percent chance (range 10 to 30) of reaching 90 percent on real layer strips, and the best untried design is two CTC heads on a shared encoder (upper and lower voice), preceded by a zero-GPU linear probe on the existing checkpoints. Proposed kill line if ever tried: layer-strip pitch recall below 85 percent on a dev set of real layer strips, or `layer.split` recall below 0.7, after both A8b and two heads; needs about 300 or more layer notes from 3 or more pieces outside the test set. Estimated 2 to 4 weeks, about 2 to 3 days of it GPU. Recommended mitigation if work resumes: flag layer strips `review_needed` (not implemented). Per the 2026-10-18 ruling, split-stem writing is played merged into its voice's track, so the effect is wrong pitches on those strips, not a pipeline failure.
